1️⃣ The Living World

1. What is Living?

Living organisms show characteristics such as growth, reproduction, metabolism, cellular organisation and response to stimuli.

Metabolism: The sum total of all chemical reactions occurring within a living organism is called metabolism.

2. Biodiversity

The variety of living organisms present on Earth is called biodiversity.

3. Taxonomy

Taxonomy is the science of identification, nomenclature and classification of organisms.

4. Systematics

Systematics includes identification, nomenclature, classification and evolutionary relationships of organisms.

5. Taxonomic Categories

Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species

6. Species

Species is the basic unit of classification. In the biological species concept, members generally share fundamental similarities and can reproduce to produce fertile offspring under natural conditions.

7. Binomial Nomenclature

Scientific names generally consist of two parts: genus and specific epithet.

Example format: Genus + specific epithet

The genus begins with a capital letter and the specific epithet begins with a small letter.

Important Questions

1. Define taxonomy.
2. What is binomial nomenclature?
3. Write the hierarchy of taxonomic categories.
2️⃣ Biological Classification

1. Five Kingdom Classification

The five-kingdom system proposed by R.H. Whittaker classified organisms into five major kingdoms.

  • Monera
  • Protista
  • Fungi
  • Plantae
  • Animalia

2. Monera

Members of Monera are prokaryotic organisms. Bacteria are important members of this group.

3. Protista

Protists are primarily unicellular eukaryotic organisms.

4. Fungi

Fungi are eukaryotic organisms with absorptive nutrition. Their cell walls commonly contain chitin.

5. Plantae

Plants are multicellular eukaryotes that generally perform photosynthesis.

6. Animalia

Animals are multicellular eukaryotes that obtain nutrition by ingestion.

Kingdom Basic Feature
Monera Prokaryotic
Protista Mostly unicellular eukaryotic
Fungi Chitinous cell wall
Plantae Multicellular photosynthetic organisms
Animalia Multicellular ingestive heterotrophs

Viruses

Viruses are acellular infectious agents that contain genetic material enclosed in a protein coat and depend on host cells for replication.

⭐ Virus is not considered a typical cellular organism.

Important Questions

1. Explain five-kingdom classification.
2. Write characteristics of Monera.
3. What are viruses?
3️⃣ Plant Kingdom

Major Plant Groups

  • Algae
  • Bryophytes
  • Pteridophytes
  • Gymnosperms
  • Angiosperms

1. Algae

Algae are mainly aquatic, photosynthetic organisms. They can be unicellular or multicellular.

2. Bryophytes

Bryophytes are small plants that generally require water for sexual reproduction and lack true vascular tissue.

3. Pteridophytes

Pteridophytes are vascular plants that reproduce by spores and do not produce seeds.

4. Gymnosperms

Gymnosperms bear naked seeds; their ovules are not enclosed within an ovary.

5. Angiosperms

Angiosperms are flowering plants in which ovules are enclosed within an ovary and seeds develop within fruits.

Plant sequence to remember: Algae → Bryophytes → Pteridophytes → Gymnosperms → Angiosperms

Important Questions

1. Differentiate gymnosperms and angiosperms.
2. Write characteristics of bryophytes.
3. What are pteridophytes?
4️⃣ Animal Kingdom

Basis of Classification

  • Level of organisation
  • Symmetry
  • Germ layers
  • Coelom
  • Segmentation
  • Presence or absence of notochord

Symmetry

Animals may show asymmetry, radial symmetry or bilateral symmetry.

Germ Layers

Diploblastic animals have two germ layers, while triploblastic animals have three germ layers.

Coelom

The body cavity lined by mesoderm is called a coelom.

Major Phyla

Phylum Important Feature

Cnidaria Cnidoblasts
Platyhelminthes Flatworms
Nematoda Roundworms
Annelida Segmented body
Arthropoda Jointed appendages
Mollusca Soft body, often with shell
Echinodermata Water vascular system
Chordata Notochord at least during development
⭐ Learn each phylum by its unique identifying feature.

Important Questions

1. Explain basis of animal classification.
2. Write features of Arthropoda.
3. What is a notochord?
5️⃣ Morphology of Flowering Plants

Root

Roots generally anchor plants and absorb water and minerals. They may also perform storage and other functions.

Types of Root Systems

  • Tap root system
  • Fibrous root system
  • Adventitious roots

Stem

The stem supports leaves, flowers and fruits and transports water, minerals and organic nutrients.

Leaf

Leaves are the main photosynthetic organs in most plants.

Leaf Parts

  • Leaf base
  • Petioles
  • Lamina

Venation

The arrangement of veins and veinlets in the leaf blade is called venation.

Inflorescence

The arrangement of flowers on the floral axis is called inflorescence.

Flower

A flower is the reproductive structure of angiosperms.

Four Floral Whorls

  • Calyx
  • Corolla
  • Androecium
  • Gynoecium

Fruit

A fruit generally develops from the ovary after fertilisation, although some fruits can develop without fertilisation.

Seed

A seed contains an embryo and food reserves in many plants.

⭐ Root → Stem → Leaf → Flower → Fruit → Seed

Important Questions

1. Explain tap root and fibrous root.
2. Name the four floral whorls.
3. What is venation?
6️⃣ Anatomy of Flowering Plants

Plant Tissues

A tissue is a group of cells having common origin and usually performing a common function.

Meristematic Tissue

Meristematic cells actively divide and help in plant growth.

  • Apical meristem
  • Intercalary meristem
  • Lateral meristem

Permanent Tissues

  • Parenchyma
  • Collenchyma
  • Sclerenchyma

Complex Tissues

Xylem and phloem are complex permanent tissues.

Tissue Main Function
Xylem Transport of water and minerals
Phloem Transport of organic nutrients

Plant Anatomy

Plant anatomy studies the internal structure of plants. Important anatomical structures include root, stem and leaf.

⭐ Xylem → mainly water and mineral transport.
⭐ Phloem → mainly food/organic solute transport.

Important Questions

1. Differentiate meristematic and permanent tissues.
2. Explain xylem and phloem.
3. What is plant anatomy?
7️⃣ Structural Organisation in Animals

Animal Tissues

  • Epithelial tissue
  • Connective tissue
  • Muscular tissue
  • Nervous tissue

Epithelial Tissue

It covers body surfaces and lines many internal organs and cavities.

Connective Tissue

Connective tissues support, connect and protect different parts of the body.

Muscular Tissue

Muscle tissue is specialised for contraction and movement.

Nervous Tissue

Nervous tissue receives and transmits signals.

Organ and Organ System

Different tissues combine to form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

⭐ Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism

Important Questions

1. Name the four major animal tissues.
2. Explain epithelial tissue.
3. What is connective tissue?
8️⃣ Cell: The Unit of Life

1. Cell

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.

2. Prokaryotic Cell

Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and generally lack membrane-bound organelles.

3. Eukaryotic Cell

Eukaryotic cells possess a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Important Organelles

Organelle Major Function
Nucleus Stores genetic material and regulates cell activities
Mitochondria Major site of aerobic cellular respiration
Ribosome Protein synthesis
Endoplasmic reticulum Synthesis and transport of cellular products
Golgi apparatus Modification, packaging and trafficking
Lysosome Intracellular digestion
Chloroplast Photosynthesis in plants and algae
Vacuole Storage and maintenance of cell conditions

Cell Membrane

The plasma membrane is selectively permeable and regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell.

Cell Wall

Plant cells have a cell wall outside the plasma membrane, mainly composed of cellulose.

⭐ Mitochondria and chloroplasts contain their own DNA and ribosomes and are considered semi-autonomous organelles.

Important Questions

1. Differentiate prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
2. Explain functions of mitochondria.
3. What is the function of ribosomes?
9️⃣ Biomolecules

1. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates include sugars, starch and related compounds. They commonly function as energy sources and structural materials.

2. Proteins

Proteins are polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. They perform structural, catalytic, transport and many other functions.

3. Amino Acids

Amino acids contain amino and carboxyl groups along with a variable side chain.

4. Lipids

Lipids include fats, oils, phospholipids and other hydrophobic or amphipathic molecules.

5. Nucleic Acids

DNA and RNA are nucleic acids that store, transmit and express genetic information.

Molecule Basic Unit
Protein Amino acids
Polysaccharide Monosaccharides
Nucleic acid Nucleotides

Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up biochemical reactions without being consumed in the overall reaction.

⭐ Enzymes are highly specific and their activity depends on factors such as temperature and pH.

Important Questions

1. What are biomolecules?
2. Define enzyme.
3. Differentiate DNA and RNA.
🔟 Cell Cycle and Cell Division

1. Cell Cycle

The cell cycle is the sequence of events through which a cell grows, duplicates its genetic material and divides.

2. Interphase

  • G₁ phase
  • S phase
  • G₂ phase

During S phase, DNA replication occurs.

3. Mitosis

Mitosis is a type of cell division that generally produces two genetically similar daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent cell.

Stages of Mitosis

  • Prophase
  • Metaphase
  • Anaphase
  • Telophase

4. Cytokinesis

Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm after nuclear division.

5. Meiosis

Meiosis is a specialised division that reduces chromosome number and produces genetically varied cells.

6. Importance of Meiosis

  • Reduction of chromosome number
  • Formation of gametes in animals
  • Genetic variation
⭐ Mitosis → growth, repair and maintenance.
⭐ Meiosis → reduction division and genetic variation.

Important Questions

1. Explain stages of mitosis.
2. Differentiate mitosis and meiosis.
3. What happens during S phase?
1️⃣1️⃣ Transport in Plants

1. Water Transport

Plants transport water and minerals mainly through xylem.

2. Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

3. Osmosis

Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.

4. Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water in the form of water vapour from aerial parts of plants, mainly through stomata.

5. Transpiration Pull

Evaporation of water from leaves creates a negative pressure that helps pull water upward through the xylem.

6. Phloem Transport

Organic nutrients such as sugars are transported through phloem from source regions to sink regions.

⭐ Xylem → mainly water and minerals.
⭐ Phloem → mainly organic solutes.

Important Questions

1. Define osmosis.
2. Explain transpiration pull.
3. Differentiate xylem and phloem transport.
1️⃣2️⃣ Mineral Nutrition

Essential Elements

Plants require several mineral elements for normal growth, development and metabolism.

Macronutrients

  • Nitrogen
  • Phosphorus
  • Potassium
  • Calcium
  • Magnesium
  • Sulphur

Micronutrients

  • Iron
  • Manganese
  • Zinc
  • Copper
  • Boron
  • Molybdenum
  • Chlorine
  • Nickel

Nitrogen

Nitrogen is required for amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids and many other biomolecules.

Nitrogen Fixation

Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into biologically useful forms is called nitrogen fixation.

Deficiency Symptoms

Deficiency of mineral elements can cause characteristic symptoms such as chlorosis, necrosis, stunted growth and poor development.

⭐ Learn each nutrient with: source → function → deficiency symptoms.

Important Questions

1. Differentiate macronutrients and micronutrients.
2. Why is nitrogen important for plants?
3. What is nitrogen fixation?
1️⃣3️⃣ Photosynthesis in Plants

1. Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and other photosynthetic organisms convert light energy into chemical energy, producing carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water.

Carbon dioxide + Water → Carbohydrate + Oxygen

Light energy and chlorophyll are required.

2. Chloroplast

Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and are the main sites of photosynthesis in plant cells.

3. Light Reaction

Light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. They involve light absorption, electron transfer and formation of ATP and NADPH.

4. Calvin Cycle

The Calvin cycle occurs in the chloroplast stroma and uses ATP and NADPH to help assimilate carbon dioxide into organic compounds.

5. Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

  • Light intensity
  • Carbon dioxide concentration
  • Temperature
  • Water availability
⭐ Light reactions → thylakoid membranes.
⭐ Calvin cycle → stroma.

Important Questions

1. Define photosynthesis.
2. Explain light-dependent reactions.
3. What is the Calvin cycle?
1️⃣4️⃣ Respiration in Plants

1. Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells release energy from organic molecules and capture part of it in usable forms such as ATP.

2. Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the breakdown of glucose into pyruvate and occurs in the cytoplasm.

3. Krebs Cycle

The Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix in eukaryotic cells under aerobic conditions.

4. Electron Transport Chain

The electron transport chain is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane and is associated with oxidative phosphorylation.

5. Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen as the terminal electron acceptor and generally produces much more ATP than fermentation.

6. Fermentation

Fermentation is an anaerobic process in which organic molecules are incompletely oxidised.

⭐ Glycolysis → cytoplasm.
⭐ Krebs cycle → mitochondrial matrix.
⭐ ETC → inner mitochondrial membrane.

Important Questions

1. Explain glycolysis.
2. Where does Krebs cycle occur?
3. Differentiate aerobic respiration and fermentation.
1️⃣5️⃣ Plant Growth and Development

1. Growth

Growth is an irreversible increase in size, dry mass or number of cells of an organism or part of it.

2. Plant Growth Regulators

  • Auxins
  • Gibberellins
  • Cytokinins
  • Ethylene
  • Abscisic acid

3. Auxins

Auxins promote cell elongation and influence processes such as apical dominance and root initiation.

4. Gibberellins

Gibberellins promote stem elongation and can influence seed germination and flowering in some plants.

5. Cytokinins

Cytokinins promote cell division and can delay leaf senescence.

6. Ethylene

Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone involved in fruit ripening and several developmental responses.

7. Abscisic Acid

ABA promotes responses associated with stress, including stomatal closure, and can contribute to seed dormancy.

Hormone Important Role
Auxin Cell elongation
Gibberellin Stem elongation
Cytokinin Cell division
Ethylene Fruit ripening
ABA Stress response and dormancy

Important Questions

1. Name five major plant growth regulators.
2. Write functions of auxin.
3. Explain the role of ethylene.
1️⃣6️⃣ Digestion and Absorption

1. Human Digestive System

The digestive system consists of the alimentary canal and associated digestive glands.

2. Alimentary Canal

Mouth → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus

3. Mouth

Digestion begins in the mouth. Saliva contains salivary amylase, which begins starch digestion.

4. Stomach

The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, pepsinogen and mucus. Acidic conditions activate pepsin and help protein digestion.

5. Small Intestine

Most digestion and absorption of nutrients occur in the small intestine.

6. Villi

Villi and microvilli greatly increase the surface area available for absorption.

7. Liver

The liver produces bile, which helps emulsify fats.

8. Pancreas

The pancreas secretes digestive enzymes and bicarbonate-rich fluid into the small intestine.

⭐ Small intestine = major site of digestion and absorption.

Important Questions

1. Explain the human digestive system.
2. What is the role of bile?
3. Why are villi important?
1️⃣7️⃣ Breathing and Exchange of Gases

1. Breathing

Breathing involves inspiration and expiration and provides air for gas exchange.

2. Respiratory System

Nostrils → Nasal cavity → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli

3. Alveoli

Alveoli are thin-walled air sacs that provide a large surface for exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

4. Inspiration

During normal inspiration, the diaphragm contracts and the thoracic volume increases, helping air enter the lungs.

5. Expiration

During normal quiet expiration, the diaphragm relaxes and thoracic volume decreases.

6. Transport of Oxygen

Most oxygen is transported in blood bound to haemoglobin.

7. Transport of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is transported in several forms, with much of it carried as bicarbonate ions.

⭐ Gas exchange occurs mainly across the alveolar-capillary surface.

Important Questions

1. Explain inspiration and expiration.
2. What is the function of alveoli?
3. How is oxygen transported in blood?
1️⃣8️⃣ Body Fluids and Circulation

1. Blood

Blood is a connective tissue consisting of plasma and formed elements.

2. Blood Components

  • Plasma
  • Red blood cells
  • White blood cells
  • Platelets

3. RBC

Red blood cells contain haemoglobin and transport oxygen.

4. WBC

White blood cells participate in defence and immunity.

5. Platelets

Platelets play an important role in blood clotting.

6. Heart

The human heart is a muscular four-chambered organ that pumps blood through pulmonary and systemic circulation.

Heart Chambers

  • Right atrium
  • Right ventricle
  • Left atrium
  • Left ventricle

7. Double Circulation

In humans, blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circuit of pulmonary and systemic circulation.

⭐ Right side → mainly deoxygenated blood.
⭐ Left side → mainly oxygenated blood.

Important Questions

1. Name components of blood.
2. Explain double circulation.
3. Write functions of RBC, WBC and platelets.
1️⃣9️⃣ Excretory Products and Their Elimination

1. Excretion

Excretion is the removal of metabolic waste products from the body.

2. Human Excretory System

  • Kidneys
  • Ureters
  • Urinary bladder
  • Urethra

3. Nephron

The nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney.

4. Urine Formation

Urine formation involves processes including glomerular filtration, selective reabsorption and tubular secretion.

5. Glomerulus

The glomerulus is a network of capillaries involved in filtration of blood.

6. Bowman's Capsule

Bowman's capsule surrounds the glomerulus and collects the filtrate.

7. ADH

Antidiuretic hormone helps regulate water reabsorption and therefore influences urine concentration.

⭐ Nephron = structural and functional unit of kidney.

Important Questions

1. Explain the structure of nephron.
2. Explain urine formation.
3. What is the role of ADH?
2️⃣0️⃣ Locomotion and Movement

1. Movement

Movement is a change in position of the body or a body part.

2. Types of Muscles

  • Skeletal muscle
  • Smooth muscle
  • Cardiac muscle

3. Skeletal Muscle

Skeletal muscles are generally striated and under voluntary control.

4. Smooth Muscle

Smooth muscles are non-striated and generally involuntary.

5. Cardiac Muscle

Cardiac muscle forms the muscular wall of the heart and contracts rhythmically.

6. Skeletal System

The skeleton provides support, protection and sites for muscle attachment.

7. Joints

Joints are places where two or more bones or cartilages meet. They allow different degrees of movement depending on their structure.

⭐ Skeletal → voluntary.
⭐ Smooth → involuntary.
⭐ Cardiac → involuntary and rhythmic.

Important Questions

1. Differentiate three types of muscles.
2. What are joints?
3. Write functions of skeletal system.
2️⃣1️⃣ Neural Control and Coordination

1. Nervous System

The nervous system receives information, processes it and coordinates responses.

2. Neuron

Neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

Parts of Neuron

  • Dendrites
  • Cell body
  • Axon

3. Brain

The brain is the major control centre of the nervous system.

4. Spinal Cord

The spinal cord connects the brain with peripheral nerves and participates in reflex actions.

5. Reflex Action

A reflex is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus.

6. Synapse

A synapse is a junction between neurons or between a neuron and an effector cell where signals are transmitted.

⭐ Neuron → basic functional unit of nervous system.

Important Questions

1. Draw and label a neuron.
2. Explain reflex action.
3. What is a synapse?
2️⃣2️⃣ Chemical Coordination and Integration

1. Endocrine System

The endocrine system consists of glands that release hormones into the blood.

2. Hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers produced by endocrine tissues that regulate physiological processes.

3. Major Endocrine Glands

  • Hypothalamus
  • Pituitary
  • Thyroid
  • Parathyroid
  • Adrenal
  • Pancreas
  • Gonads

4. Pituitary

The pituitary is closely connected with the hypothalamus and regulates several other endocrine glands and physiological functions.

5. Thyroid

Thyroid hormones help regulate metabolism, growth and development.

6. Adrenal Glands

Adrenal hormones participate in stress responses, metabolism and electrolyte balance.

7. Pancreas

The endocrine pancreas produces hormones such as insulin and glucagon that help regulate blood glucose.

⭐ Hormones act as chemical messengers.

Important Questions

1. What are hormones?
2. Write functions of thyroid hormones.
3. Explain endocrine glands.
