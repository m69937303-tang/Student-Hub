Class 11 Chemistry — Detailed Notes

Concepts • Formulas • Reactions • Important Questions

1️⃣ Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

1. Matter

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. Matter can exist mainly as solids, liquids and gases.

2. Laws of Chemical Combination

Law of Conservation of Mass

Mass is neither created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.

Law of Definite Proportions

A pure chemical compound always contains the same elements in a fixed proportion by mass.

3. Mole Concept

A mole is the amount of substance containing approximately 6.022 × 10²³ specified particles.

1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles

Number of moles = Given mass / Molar mass

n = m/M

4. Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance. Its common unit is g mol⁻¹.

5. Molarity

Molarity = Moles of solute / Volume of solution in litres

6. Molality

Molality = Moles of solute / Mass of solvent in kg

7. Mole Fraction

Xᵢ = Number of moles of component i / Total number of moles

8. Empirical Formula

The empirical formula represents the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound.

9. Molecular Formula

Molecular formula = (Empirical formula) × n

10. Limiting Reagent

The reactant that gets completely consumed first and limits the amount of product formed is called the limiting reagent.

⭐ Mole concept is extremely important for numerical questions.

Important Questions

1. Define mole and Avogadro constant.
2. Differentiate molarity and molality.
3. What is limiting reagent?
2️⃣ Structure of Atom

1. Fundamental Particles

Particle Charge Relative Mass
Electron −1 Very small
Proton +1 Approximately 1 u
Neutron 0 Approximately 1 u

2. Atomic Number

Z = Number of protons

For a neutral atom, number of electrons equals number of protons.

3. Mass Number

A = Number of protons + Number of neutrons

4. Isotopes

Atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers are isotopes.

5. Bohr Model

According to Bohr's model, electrons occupy certain permitted stationary energy levels around the nucleus.

6. Electromagnetic Radiation

c = νλ

E = hν

Here c is speed of light, ν is frequency and λ is wavelength.

7. Quantum Numbers

  • Principal quantum number (n)
  • Azimuthal quantum number (l)
  • Magnetic quantum number (m)
  • Spin quantum number (s)

8. Orbitals

An orbital is a region of space where the probability of finding an electron is high.

9. Electronic Configuration

Electrons are distributed among orbitals according to energy and quantum-mechanical rules.

⭐ Remember: Atomic number = number of protons.

Important Questions

1. Explain Bohr's atomic model.
2. What are quantum numbers?
3. Differentiate orbit and orbital.
3️⃣ Classification of Elements and Periodicity

1. Modern Periodic Law

The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

2. Periods

The horizontal rows of the periodic table are called periods.

3. Groups

The vertical columns are called groups.

4. Atomic Radius

Atomic radius generally decreases across a period and increases down a group, with common periodic trends.

5. Ionisation Enthalpy

It is the enthalpy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

6. Electron Gain Enthalpy

It describes the enthalpy change associated with addition of an electron to an isolated gaseous atom.

7. Electronegativity

Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract the shared electron pair towards itself.

Property Across Period Down Group
Atomic radius Generally decreases Generally increases
Ionisation enthalpy Generally increases Generally decreases
Electronegativity Generally increases Generally decreases
⭐ Periodic trends are very important for reasoning questions.

Important Questions

1. State modern periodic law.
2. Explain periodic trends in atomic radius.
3. Define ionisation enthalpy and electronegativity.
4️⃣ Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

1. Chemical Bond

A chemical bond is an attractive interaction that holds atoms or ions together in a stable arrangement.

2. Ionic Bond

An ionic bond results from electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions, usually formed after electron transfer.

3. Covalent Bond

A covalent bond involves sharing of electron pairs between atoms.

4. Lewis Structure

Lewis structures represent valence electrons and bonding between atoms.

5. Octet Rule

Many atoms tend to achieve a stable arrangement of eight valence electrons, although there are important exceptions.

6. VSEPR Theory

Valence shell electron pairs repel each other and arrange themselves to minimise repulsion.

7. Hybridisation

Hybridisation Common Geometry
sp Linear
sp² Trigonal planar
sp³ Tetrahedral
sp³d Trigonal bipyramidal
sp³d² Octahedral

8. Hydrogen Bond

A hydrogen bond is an attractive interaction involving hydrogen attached to a highly electronegative atom and another electronegative atom.

9. Molecular Polarity

A molecule can be polar when its bond dipoles do not cancel because of its geometry.

⭐ Learn Lewis structures + VSEPR + hybridisation together.

Important Questions

1. Explain ionic and covalent bonding.
2. Explain VSEPR theory.
3. Explain hybridisation with examples.
5️⃣ Thermodynamics

1. System

The portion of the universe selected for thermodynamic study is called the system.

2. Surroundings

Everything outside the system is called the surroundings.

3. Internal Energy

Internal energy is the total energy associated with the microscopic state of a system.

4. Enthalpy

H = U + PV

5. First Law of Thermodynamics

ΔU = q + w

The exact signs depend on the chemistry sign convention being used.

6. Exothermic Reaction

A reaction that releases heat to the surroundings is exothermic.

For an exothermic process: ΔH < 0

7. Endothermic Reaction

A reaction that absorbs heat from the surroundings is endothermic.

For an endothermic process: ΔH > 0

8. Hess's Law

The enthalpy change of a reaction is the same whether the reaction occurs in one step or several steps, provided initial and final states are the same.

⭐ Thermodynamics is important for both concepts and numericals.

Important Questions

1. State the first law of thermodynamics.
2. Differentiate exothermic and endothermic reactions.
3. Explain Hess's law.
6️⃣ Equilibrium

1. Chemical Equilibrium

Chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state in a reversible reaction where the rates of forward and reverse reactions become equal.

2. Equilibrium Constant

For a general reaction:

aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ

3. Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it tends to shift in a direction that opposes the disturbance.

4. Acids and Bases

Acids and bases can be described using different theories, including Arrhenius and Brønsted-Lowry concepts.

5. pH

pH = −log[H⁺]

pOH = −log[OH⁻]

At 25°C: pH + pOH = 14

6. Ionic Equilibrium

Ionic equilibrium deals with equilibrium involving ions, especially in weak electrolytes and aqueous solutions.

⭐ Le Chatelier's principle is frequently used in conceptual questions.

Important Questions

1. Define dynamic equilibrium.
2. State Le Chatelier's principle.
3. Define pH.
7️⃣ Redox Reactions

1. Oxidation

Oxidation can be described as loss of electrons, increase in oxidation number, or addition of oxygen/removal of hydrogen in suitable reactions.

2. Reduction

Reduction can be described as gain of electrons, decrease in oxidation number, or removal of oxygen/addition of hydrogen in suitable reactions.

OIL → Oxidation Is Loss
RIG → Reduction Is Gain

3. Oxidising Agent

An oxidising agent causes oxidation of another species and itself undergoes reduction.

4. Reducing Agent

A reducing agent causes reduction of another species and itself undergoes oxidation.

5. Oxidation Number

Oxidation number is a bookkeeping number assigned to an atom according to standard rules.

6. Redox Reaction

A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously is called a redox reaction.

⭐ Oxidation and reduction always occur together in a redox reaction.

Important Questions

1. Define oxidation and reduction.
2. What is an oxidising agent?
3. Explain oxidation number.
8️⃣ Organic Chemistry — Some Basic Principles

1. Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry mainly deals with carbon compounds, especially compounds containing carbon-hydrogen frameworks.

2. Tetravalency of Carbon

Carbon generally forms four covalent bonds because it has four valence electrons.

3. Catenation

The ability of carbon atoms to form bonds with other carbon atoms to make chains and rings is called catenation.

4. Functional Group

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms responsible for characteristic chemical behaviour of an organic compound.

Functional Group Example Class
−OH Alcohol
−CHO Aldehyde
>C=O Ketone
−COOH Carboxylic acid
−NH₂ Amine

5. Homologous Series

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds having the same functional group and general chemical properties, with successive members differing by −CH₂−.

6. IUPAC Nomenclature

  1. Select the longest appropriate carbon chain.
  2. Number the chain according to the rules.
  3. Identify substituents.
  4. Assign their positions.
  5. Write the final name.

7. Isomerism

Isomers have the same molecular formula but different arrangements or connectivity of atoms.

⭐ IUPAC naming is a skill—practice many examples.

Important Questions

1. What is catenation?
2. What is a functional group?
3. Explain homologous series.
9️⃣ Hydrocarbons

1. Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

2. Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons containing only single carbon-carbon bonds.

General formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

3. Alkenes

Alkenes contain at least one carbon-carbon double bond.

General formula for open-chain monoalkenes: CₙH₂ₙ

4. Alkynes

Alkynes contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond.

General formula for open-chain monoalkynes: CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

5. Aromatic Hydrocarbons

Benzene is the simplest and most important aromatic hydrocarbon.

6. Combustion

Hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Heat

7. Addition Reactions

Unsaturated hydrocarbons can undergo addition reactions across carbon-carbon multiple bonds.

8. Substitution Reactions

Alkanes commonly undergo substitution reactions under appropriate conditions.

⭐ Learn the difference between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.

Important Questions

1. Differentiate alkane, alkene and alkyne.
2. What is an aromatic hydrocarbon?
3. Explain addition reaction.
🔟 Some Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry — Quick Revision

Important Organic Terms

  • Electrophile: Electron-deficient species that accepts an electron pair.
  • Nucleophile: Electron-rich species that donates an electron pair.
  • Homolytic cleavage: Bond breaking in which each atom gets one electron.
  • Heterolytic cleavage: Bond breaking in which one atom gets both bonding electrons.
  • Carbocation: Carbon-containing species with a positively charged carbon.
  • Carbanion: Carbon-containing species with a negatively charged carbon.
  • Free radical: Species containing an unpaired electron.

Electronic Effects

  • Inductive effect
  • Resonance effect
  • Electromeric effect
  • Hyperconjugation
⭐ These concepts become very important in higher-level organic chemistry.
