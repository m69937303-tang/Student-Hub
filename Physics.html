1️⃣ Electric Charges and Fields

1. Electric Charge

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for electrical interaction.

  • SI unit → Coulomb (C)
  • Charge of electron → −1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  • Charge of proton → +1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

2. Properties of Charge

  • Conservation of charge
  • Quantisation of charge
  • Additivity of charge
q = ne

3. Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

F = (1/4πε₀) × (q₁q₂/r²)

4. Electric Field

Electric field at a point is the force experienced by a unit positive test charge placed at that point.

E = F/q

For a point charge:

E = (1/4πε₀) × q/r²

5. Superposition Principle

The net electric field due to several charges is the vector sum of the electric fields produced by individual charges.

E⃗net = E⃗₁ + E⃗₂ + E⃗₃ + ...

6. Electric Dipole

An electric dipole consists of two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.

p = q × 2a

Direction of dipole moment is from negative charge to positive charge.

7. Torque on Dipole

τ = pE sinθ

8. Electric Flux

Φ = E A cosθ

9. Gauss's Law

Φ = q enclosed / ε₀

Gauss's law relates the electric flux through a closed surface to the net charge enclosed by that surface.

10. Applications of Gauss's Law

  • Infinite line charge
  • Infinite plane sheet
  • Spherical shell

Infinite Line Charge

E = λ / (2πε₀r)

Infinite Plane Sheet

E = σ / (2ε₀)
⭐ Important derivations: Electric field due to infinite line charge, plane sheet and spherical shell.

🔥 Important Questions

2. Define electric field and electric flux.
3. Explain Gauss's law.
4. Derive electric field due to an infinite plane sheet.
5. Derive torque on an electric dipole.
2️⃣ Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

1. Electric Potential

Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive test charge in bringing it from infinity to that point.

V = W/q

2. Potential Due to Point Charge

V = (1/4πε₀) × q/r

3. Potential Difference

ΔV = W/q

4. Relation Between Electric Field and Potential

E = −dV/dr

5. Potential Energy

Potential energy of two point charges is:

U = (1/4πε₀) × q₁q₂/r

6. Electric Dipole Potential

V = (1/4πε₀) × (p cosθ/r²)

7. Capacitance

Capacitance is the ability of a conductor to store electric charge.

C = Q/V

8. Parallel Plate Capacitor

C = ε₀A/d

With dielectric:

C = Kε₀A/d

9. Combination of Capacitors

Parallel

Ceq = C₁ + C₂ + C₃ + ...

Series

1/Ceq = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃ + ...

10. Energy Stored in Capacitor

U = ½CV²

Other forms:

U = ½QV = Q²/(2C)
⭐ Inserting a dielectric between capacitor plates increases capacitance.

🔥 Important Questions

1. Define electric potential.
2. Derive capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor.
3. Derive energy stored in a capacitor.
4. Explain series and parallel combination of capacitors.
3️⃣ Current Electricity

1. Electric Current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

I = Q/t

2. Drift Velocity

The average velocity acquired by free electrons in a conductor due to an applied electric field is called drift velocity.

I = neAvd

3. Ohm's Law

V = IR

At constant physical conditions, current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

4. Resistance

R = ρL/A

Resistance depends on length, area, material and temperature.

5. Resistivity

Resistivity is a property of the material of the conductor.

ρ = RA/L

6. Series Combination

Req = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...

7. Parallel Combination

1/Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...

8. Electrical Power

P = VI
P = I²R
P = V²/R

9. Kirchhoff's Laws

Junction Rule

ΣI = 0

Total current entering a junction equals total current leaving it.

Loop Rule

ΣΔV = 0

The algebraic sum of potential changes around a closed loop is zero.

10. Wheatstone Bridge

P/Q = R/S

At balance, no current flows through the galvanometer.

11. Meter Bridge

A meter bridge works on the principle of Wheatstone bridge and is used to determine unknown resistance.

⭐ Important numerical topics: series-parallel resistance, Kirchhoff laws, Wheatstone bridge and electrical power.

🔥 Important Questions

1. State and explain Ohm's law.
2. Derive R = ρL/A.
3. State Kirchhoff's laws.
4. Explain Wheatstone bridge.
4️⃣ Moving Charges and Magnetism

1. Magnetic Force

A charged particle moving in a magnetic field experiences a magnetic force.

F = qvB sinθ

If the velocity is perpendicular to the magnetic field:

F = qvB

2. Force on Current Carrying Wire

F = BIL sinθ

3. Motion of Charged Particle

When a charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to its velocity, it moves in a circular path.

r = mv/qB

4. Time Period

T = 2πm/qB

5. Biot-Savart Law

dB = (μ₀/4π) × (I dl sinθ/r²)

6. Magnetic Field Due to Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I/(2πr)

7. Force Between Parallel Currents

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd)

8. Torque on Current Loop

τ = NIAB sinθ

9. Moving Coil Galvanometer

A moving coil galvanometer is used to detect and measure small currents.

τ = NIAB

10. Conversion of Galvanometer

  • Galvanometer → Ammeter using low resistance shunt
  • Galvanometer → Voltmeter using high resistance in series
⭐ Remember: A magnetic field can change the direction of velocity of a charged particle but does no work on the particle.

🔥 Important Questions

1. Derive the radius of circular motion of a charged particle in magnetic field.
2. State Biot-Savart law.
3. Derive force between two parallel current-carrying conductors.
4. Explain moving coil galvanometer.
5️⃣ Magnetism and Matter

1. Magnetic Field

The region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic effects can be observed is called a magnetic field.

2. Magnetic Dipole

A bar magnet behaves approximately like a magnetic dipole.

3. Magnetic Dipole Moment

M = m × 2l

4. Torque on Magnetic Dipole

τ = MB sinθ

5. Potential Energy

U = −MB cosθ

6. Types of Magnetic Materials

  • Diamagnetic
  • Paramagnetic
  • Ferromagnetic

Diamagnetic

Weakly repelled by a magnetic field.

Paramagnetic

Weakly attracted by a magnetic field.

Ferromagnetic

Strongly attracted by a magnetic field and can retain magnetisation.

⭐ Examples: Diamagnetic → copper, Paramagnetic → aluminium, Ferromagnetic → iron.

7. Earth as a Magnet

Earth has its own magnetic field. The magnetic elements include declination, dip and horizontal component.

🔥 Important Questions

1. Define magnetic dipole moment.
2. Explain diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferromagnetic substances.
3. Derive torque acting on a magnetic dipole.
6️⃣ Electromagnetic Induction

1. Electromagnetic Induction

The phenomenon of production of induced emf due to a change in magnetic flux linked with a circuit is called electromagnetic induction.

2. Magnetic Flux

Φ = BA cosθ

3. Faraday's Law

ε = −dΦ/dt

The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

4. Lenz's Law

The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change in magnetic flux responsible for producing it.

5. Motional EMF

ε = Blv

6. Self Inductance

ε = −L(di/dt)

7. Energy Stored in Inductor

U = ½LI²
⭐ Lenz's law follows from conservation of energy.
7️⃣ Alternating Current

AC Voltage

V = V₀ sinωt

RMS Value

Vrms = V₀/√2
Irms = I₀/√2

Reactance

Inductive Reactance

XL = ωL

Capacitive Reactance

XC = 1/ωC

Impedance

Z = √[R² + (XL − XC)²]

Power in AC Circuit

P = Vrms Irms cosφ

Resonance

In a series LCR circuit, resonance occurs when inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance.

XL = XC
ω₀ = 1/√LC
8️⃣ Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields which are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.

c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)

Electromagnetic Spectrum

  1. Radio waves
  2. Microwaves
  3. Infrared
  4. Visible light
  5. Ultraviolet
  6. X-rays
  7. Gamma rays
⭐ Electromagnetic waves do not require a material medium for propagation.
9️⃣ Ray Optics and Optical Instruments

1. Reflection

Reflection is the bouncing back of light from a surface.

i = r

2. Mirror Formula

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

3. Magnification

m = −v/u

4. Refraction

n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r

This is Snell's law.

5. Lens Formula

1/f = 1/v − 1/u

6. Lens Magnification

m = v/u

7. Power of Lens

P = 1/f

Here f must be in metres and power is measured in dioptre.

8. Total Internal Reflection

When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, total internal reflection occurs.

9. Optical Instruments

  • Microscope
  • Telescope
  • Human eye
⭐ Important topics: mirror formula, lens formula, refraction, total internal reflection and optical instruments.
🔟 Wave Optics

1. Wave Nature of Light

Wave optics explains phenomena such as interference, diffraction and polarisation.

2. Interference

Interference is the redistribution of intensity produced when two coherent waves superpose.

3. Young's Double Slit Experiment

β = λD/d

β is fringe width.

4. Constructive Interference

Path difference = nλ

5. Destructive Interference

Path difference = (2n+1)λ/2

6. Diffraction

Diffraction is the bending/spreading of waves around obstacles or through narrow apertures.

7. Polarisation

Polarisation demonstrates the transverse nature of light.

⭐ Young's double slit experiment is an important proof of the wave nature of light.
1️⃣1️⃣ Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter

Photoelectric Effect

The emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of suitable frequency falls on it is called photoelectric effect.

Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

hν = φ + Kmax
Kmax = hν − φ

Work Function

Minimum energy required to remove an electron from a metal surface.

φ = hν₀

de Broglie Wavelength

λ = h/p
λ = h/mv
⭐ Increasing intensity increases photoelectric current when frequency is above threshold, while maximum kinetic energy depends on frequency.
1️⃣2️⃣ Atoms

Bohr Model

Bohr proposed that electrons revolve around the nucleus in certain allowed stationary orbits.

Bohr's Angular Momentum Condition

mvr = nh/2π

Radius of nth Orbit

rn ∝ n²

Energy of Hydrogen Atom

En = −13.6/n² eV

Hydrogen Spectrum

1/λ = R(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)

where n₂ > n₁.

1️⃣3️⃣ Nuclei

1. Atomic Nucleus

The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. Together they are called nucleons.

2. Nuclear Size

R = R₀A^(1/3)

3. Mass Defect

The difference between the sum of masses of separate nucleons and the actual mass of the nucleus is called mass defect.

4. Binding Energy

E = Δmc²

5. Radioactive Decay

N = N₀e^(−λt)

6. Half Life

T½ = 0.693/λ

7. Nuclear Fission

A heavy nucleus splits into lighter nuclei with release of energy.

8. Nuclear Fusion

Two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus with release of energy.

⭐ Fusion powers stars, while fission is used in nuclear reactors.
1️⃣4️⃣ Semiconductor Electronics

1. Semiconductor

A semiconductor has electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator.

2. Intrinsic Semiconductor

A pure semiconductor without intentional impurity is called intrinsic.

3. Extrinsic Semiconductor

A semiconductor whose conductivity is increased by adding suitable impurities is called extrinsic semiconductor.

4. n-Type

Pentavalent impurity produces an n-type semiconductor. Electrons are the majority carriers.

5. p-Type

Trivalent impurity produces a p-type semiconductor. Holes are the majority carriers.

6. p-n Junction

A p-n junction is formed when p-type and n-type semiconductor regions are joined.

7. Diode

A diode allows current mainly in one direction.

8. LED

A light-emitting diode emits light when forward biased.

9. Solar Cell

A solar cell converts light energy into electrical energy using the photovoltaic effect.

10. Logic Gates

  • AND
  • OR
  • NOT
  • NAND
  • NOR
⭐ NAND and NOR are called universal logic gates because other basic logic functions can be constructed using them.
