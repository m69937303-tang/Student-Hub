📌 1. Electric Charge

Electric charge is a physical property of matter because of which it experiences electric force.

  • There are two types of charge: positive and negative.
  • Like charges repel each other.
  • Unlike charges attract each other.
  • SI unit of charge is Coulomb (C).

📌 2. Properties of Electric Charge

  • Quantisation: q = ne
  • Conservation: Total charge of an isolated system remains constant.
  • Additivity: Total charge is the algebraic sum of individual charges.
q = ne

Here, n = integer and e = magnitude of electronic charge.

📌 3. Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

F = (1 / 4πε₀) × (q₁q₂ / r²)

In vacuum, 1 / 4πε₀ = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻².

📌 4. Principle of Superposition

The net force on a charge due to several charges is the vector sum of the individual forces produced by each charge.

Fnet = F₁ + F₂ + F₃ + ...

📌 5. Electric Field

Electric field at a point is the force experienced by a unit positive test charge placed at that point.

E = F / q₀

SI unit: N/C or V/m

📌 6. Electric Field Due to a Point Charge

E = (1 / 4πε₀) × (q / r²)

Direction of electric field is away from a positive charge and towards a negative charge.

📌 7. Electric Field Lines

  • Electric field lines start from positive charge.
  • They end on negative charge.
  • Two electric field lines never intersect.
  • Closer lines represent a stronger electric field.
  • Field lines are perpendicular to the surface of a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium.

📌 8. Electric Dipole

An electric dipole consists of two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.

p = q × 2a

SI unit of dipole moment: C m

📌 9. Electric Flux

Electric flux represents the number of electric field lines passing through a surface.

Φ = E A cosθ

SI unit: N m²/C

📌 10. Gauss's Law

The total electric flux through a closed surface is equal to 1/ε₀ times the total charge enclosed by the surface.

Φ = qenclosed / ε₀
⭐ Gauss's law is especially useful for finding electric fields when there is high symmetry, such as spherical, cylindrical or planar symmetry.
