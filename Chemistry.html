1️⃣ Solutions

1. What is a Solution?

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more chemically non-reacting components. The component present in larger amount is called the solvent, while the component present in smaller amount is called the solute.

Example

In a salt-water solution, water is the solvent and salt is the solute.

2. Types of Solutions

  • Solid in solid — alloys
  • Solid in liquid — salt in water
  • Gas in liquid — CO₂ in water
  • Liquid in liquid — alcohol in water
  • Gas in gas — air

3. Concentration of Solution

Molarity

Molarity is the number of moles of solute present in one litre of solution.

M = n / V

where n = moles of solute and V = volume of solution in litre.

⚠️ Molarity depends on temperature because volume changes with temperature.

Molality

Molality is the number of moles of solute present in one kilogram of solvent.

m = n / mass of solvent in kg
⭐ Molality does not change with temperature because mass does not change.

Mole Fraction

X₁ = n₁ / (n₁ + n₂)

For a binary solution:

X₁ + X₂ = 1

4. Vapour Pressure

The pressure exerted by vapour of a liquid when it is in equilibrium with its liquid phase at a given temperature is called vapour pressure.

5. Raoult's Law

For a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each component is directly proportional to its mole fraction.

P₁ = X₁P₁°

For a binary solution:

Ptotal = X₁P₁° + X₂P₂°

6. Ideal Solution

An ideal solution obeys Raoult's law over the entire concentration range.

  • ΔHmix = 0
  • ΔVmix = 0
  • A-B interactions ≈ A-A and B-B interactions

7. Non-Ideal Solution

A solution that does not obey Raoult's law is called a non-ideal solution.

Positive Deviation

A-B interactions are weaker than A-A and B-B interactions. Vapour pressure becomes higher than expected.

Negative Deviation

A-B interactions are stronger than A-A and B-B interactions. Vapour pressure becomes lower than expected.

8. Colligative Properties

Properties which depend only on the number of solute particles and not on their nature are called colligative properties.

  • Relative lowering of vapour pressure
  • Elevation of boiling point
  • Depression of freezing point
  • Osmotic pressure

Elevation of Boiling Point

ΔTb = Kb × m

Depression of Freezing Point

ΔTf = Kf × m

Osmotic Pressure

π = CRT

9. Van't Hoff Factor

Van't Hoff factor represents the actual number of particles in solution relative to the number expected from the formula of the solute.

i = observed colligative property / calculated colligative property
⭐ Dissociation generally gives i > 1.
⭐ Association generally gives i < 1.

2️⃣ Electrochemistry

1. Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry deals with the relationship between electrical energy and chemical reactions.

2. Electrochemical Cell

An electrochemical cell is a device in which chemical energy can be converted into electrical energy or electrical energy can drive a chemical reaction.

3. Galvanic Cell

A galvanic cell converts energy released by a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy.

Important Rules

  • Anode → Oxidation
  • Cathode → Reduction
  • Electrons move from anode to cathode.
⭐ Remember: AN OX, RED CAT — Anode Oxidation, Reduction Cathode.

4. Electrode Potential

The tendency of an electrode to lose or gain electrons is represented by electrode potential.

5. Standard Electrode Potential

Standard electrode potential is measured under standard conditions with respect to the standard hydrogen electrode.

6. Cell Potential

E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode

7. Nernst Equation

E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q

At 298 K, this form is commonly used for electrochemical calculations.

8. Gibbs Energy and Cell Potential

ΔG° = −nFE°

For a spontaneous cell reaction, E°cell is positive and ΔG° is negative.

9. Conductance

Conductance is the reciprocal of resistance.

G = 1/R

10. Conductivity

κ = G × l/A

where l is length and A is cross-sectional area.

11. Molar Conductivity

Λm = κ × 1000 / C

12. Kohlrausch's Law

At infinite dilution, each ion contributes independently to the total molar conductivity of the electrolyte.

Λm° = λ°cation + λ°anion

13. Faraday's Laws

The amount of substance deposited during electrolysis is proportional to the quantity of electricity passed.

Q = It
m = ZIt

3️⃣ Chemical Kinetics

1. Chemical Reaction Rate

The rate of a chemical reaction is the change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time.

Rate = Change in concentration / Change in time

2. Factors Affecting Rate

  • Concentration of reactants
  • Temperature
  • Presence of catalyst
  • Nature of reactants
  • Surface area

3. Rate Law

Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n

Here k is the rate constant and m and n are experimentally determined powers.

4. Order of Reaction

Order = m + n

Order of reaction is determined experimentally from the rate law.

5. Molecularity

Molecularity is the number of reacting species taking part in an elementary reaction step.

⭐ Order is determined experimentally, while molecularity is related to an elementary reaction mechanism.

6. Zero Order Reaction

Rate = k
[R] = [R]₀ − kt

7. First Order Reaction

Rate = k[R]
k = (2.303/t) log([R]₀/[R])

8. Half Life of First Order Reaction

t½ = 0.693/k

For a first-order reaction, half-life is independent of the initial concentration.

9. Arrhenius Equation

k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)

Ea is activation energy, A is the frequency factor, R is the gas constant and T is absolute temperature.

10. Catalyst

A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy. It does not change the overall thermodynamic equilibrium constant.

⭐ Higher temperature usually increases the fraction of molecules having energy equal to or greater than activation energy.

4️⃣ d- and f-Block Elements

1. Transition Elements

Transition elements are elements whose atoms or ions have partially filled d-orbitals.

2. General Electronic Configuration

(n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns⁰⁻²

3. Important Properties

  • Variable oxidation states
  • Formation of coloured compounds
  • Complex formation
  • Magnetic behaviour
  • Catalytic activity
  • Formation of alloys

4. Variable Oxidation States

The energies of ns and (n−1)d orbitals are close to each other. Therefore, electrons from both can participate in bonding.

5. Colour

Many transition metal ions are coloured because electrons can undergo d-d transitions by absorbing certain wavelengths of visible light.

⭐ Ions with completely filled or completely empty d-subshells often do not show d-d transitions.

6. Magnetic Properties

Unpaired electrons produce paramagnetism. More unpaired electrons generally means stronger paramagnetic behaviour.

7. Catalytic Activity

Transition metals can act as catalysts because they can show variable oxidation states and can provide suitable surfaces or intermediates.

8. Lanthanides

Lanthanides involve filling of the 4f subshell.

9. Lanthanide Contraction

The gradual decrease in atomic and ionic radii across the lanthanide series is called lanthanide contraction.

10. Actinides

Actinides involve filling of the 5f subshell and many actinides are radioactive.

5️⃣ Coordination Compounds

1. Coordination Compound

A coordination compound contains a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands that are attached through coordinate bonds.

2. Ligand

A ligand is an ion or molecule capable of donating an electron pair to a central metal atom or ion.

Common Ligands

  • NH₃ → ammine
  • H₂O → aqua
  • Cl⁻ → chlorido
  • CN⁻ → cyanido
  • OH⁻ → hydroxido

3. Coordination Number

The number of donor atoms directly attached to the central metal ion is called coordination number.

4. Coordination Sphere

The central metal ion and the ligands directly attached to it are written inside square brackets and form the coordination sphere.

5. Oxidation State

The oxidation state of the central metal is calculated by considering the overall charge of the coordination compound and the charges of the ligands.

6. Werner's Theory

Werner proposed that a metal in a coordination compound has two types of valencies:

  • Primary valency → oxidation state
  • Secondary valency → coordination number

7. Coordination Isomerism

Isomerism occurs when compounds have the same overall composition but different arrangements of ligands or ions.

8. Geometrical Isomerism

Ligands can occupy different relative positions around the central metal ion, producing cis/trans or related arrangements.

9. Importance

  • Biological systems
  • Medicines
  • Metallurgy
  • Qualitative analysis
  • Industrial processes
⭐ Haemoglobin, chlorophyll and vitamin B₁₂ are examples of important biologically relevant coordination compounds or metal complexes.
