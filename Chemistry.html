Class 12 Chemistry - Detailed Notes

1️⃣ Solutions

📌 Basic Concepts

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more components. The component present in larger amount is generally called the solvent and the component present in smaller amount is called the solute.

📌 Concentration Terms

Molarity

M = Number of moles of solute / Volume of solution in litre

Molality

m = Number of moles of solute / Mass of solvent in kg

Mole Fraction

χ = Moles of component / Total moles of all components

📌 Raoult's Law

For a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each component is proportional to its mole fraction.

P₁ = χ₁P₁°

📌 Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure

(P° - P) / P° = χ₂

📌 Colligative Properties

  • Relative lowering of vapour pressure
  • Elevation in boiling point
  • Depression in freezing point
  • Osmotic pressure

Elevation in Boiling Point

ΔTb = Kb × m

Depression in Freezing Point

ΔTf = Kf × m

Osmotic Pressure

π = CRT
⭐ Important: Colligative properties depend mainly on the number of solute particles, not on their chemical nature.
2️⃣ Electrochemistry

📌 Electrochemical Cell

An electrochemical cell converts chemical energy into electrical energy or electrical energy into chemical energy.

📌 Galvanic Cell

A galvanic cell produces electrical energy from a spontaneous redox reaction.

  • Anode → Oxidation
  • Cathode → Reduction
  • Electrons flow from anode to cathode.

📌 Cell Potential

E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode

📌 Nernst Equation

E = E° - (0.0591/n) log Q

📌 Gibbs Energy

ΔG° = -nFE°

📌 Faraday's Law

The amount of substance deposited during electrolysis is proportional to the quantity of electricity passed.

m = ZIt
⭐ Remember: Oxidation always occurs at the anode and reduction always occurs at the cathode.
3️⃣ Chemical Kinetics

📌 Chemical Reaction Rate

The rate of reaction represents the change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time.

Rate = Change in concentration / Change in time

📌 Rate Law

Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ

📌 Order of Reaction

Overall order = m + n

📌 First Order Reaction

k = (2.303/t) log(a/(a-x))

📌 Half Life

t½ = 0.693/k

📌 Arrhenius Equation

k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)
⭐ Temperature increase generally increases the reaction rate because more molecules acquire energy equal to or greater than activation energy.
4️⃣ d- and f-Block Elements

📌 Transition Elements

Elements in which the differentiating electron enters the d-orbital of the penultimate shell are called transition elements.

📌 Important Properties

  • Variable oxidation states
  • Formation of coloured ions
  • Complex formation
  • Catalytic activity
  • Magnetic behaviour

📌 Why are transition metal compounds coloured?

Many transition metal ions contain partially filled d-orbitals. Electronic transitions between these orbitals can absorb visible light, producing colour.

📌 Lanthanides

Lanthanides involve filling of the 4f orbitals.

📌 Actinides

Actinides involve filling of the 5f orbitals and many of them are radioactive.

⭐ Important: Variable oxidation states are common because the energies of ns and (n-1)d orbitals are relatively close.
5️⃣ Coordination Compounds

📌 Coordination Compound

A coordination compound contains a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ions or molecules called ligands.

📌 Ligand

A ligand is an ion or molecule capable of donating an electron pair to the central metal ion.

Examples

  • NH₃ → Ammine ligand
  • H₂O → Aqua ligand
  • Cl⁻ → Chlorido ligand
  • CN⁻ → Cyanido ligand

📌 Coordination Number

The number of donor atoms directly attached to the central metal ion is called coordination number.

📌 Important Terms

  • Central metal atom/ion
  • Ligand
  • Coordination sphere
  • Coordination number
  • Oxidation state
⭐ Coordination compounds are important in biological systems, metallurgy, medicines and analytical chemistry.
6️⃣ Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

📌 Definition

Haloalkanes are compounds in which a halogen atom is attached to an alkyl group. Haloarenes contain a halogen attached directly to an aromatic ring.

📌 Important Halogens

  • F → Fluorine
  • Cl → Chlorine
  • Br → Bromine
  • I → Iodine

📌 Nucleophilic Substitution

A nucleophile replaces the halogen atom in a haloalkane.

R-X + OH⁻ → R-OH + X⁻

📌 SN1 Reaction

  • Two-step mechanism
  • Carbocation intermediate formed
  • Favoured by tertiary haloalkanes

📌 SN2 Reaction

  • One-step reaction
  • Backside attack
  • Favoured by primary haloalkanes
⭐ SN1 → Carbocation formation ⭐ SN2 → Backside attack
7️⃣ Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

📌 Alcohols

Alcohols contain the hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a saturated carbon atom.

📌 Phenols

Phenols contain an -OH group directly attached to an aromatic ring.

📌 Ethers

Ethers contain an oxygen atom between two alkyl or aryl groups.

R-OH + Na → R-ONa + ½H₂

📌 Oxidation of Alcohols

Primary alcohols can be oxidised to aldehydes and further to carboxylic acids. Secondary alcohols generally form ketones on oxidation.

⭐ Phenol is more acidic than alcohol because the phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance.
8️⃣ Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

📌 Aldehydes

Aldehydes contain the -CHO functional group.

📌 Ketones

Ketones contain the carbonyl group between two carbon groups.

📌 Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids contain the -COOH functional group.

📌 Important Reactions

Aldehyde + [O] → Carboxylic acid
Ketone + [H] → Secondary alcohol

📌 Tollens' Test

Aldehydes can reduce Tollens' reagent and give a silver deposit.

📌 Fehling's Test

Many aliphatic aldehydes give a characteristic positive Fehling's test.

⭐ Aldehydes are generally more reactive towards nucleophilic addition than ketones because of steric and electronic effects.
9️⃣ Amines

📌 Amines

Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups.

📌 Classification

  • Primary amine → RNH₂
  • Secondary amine → R₂NH
  • Tertiary amine → R₃N

📌 Basic Nature

Amines show basic character because nitrogen contains a lone pair of electrons.

📌 Important Reaction

R-NH₂ + HCl → R-NH₃⁺Cl⁻
⭐ Aliphatic amines are generally more basic than ammonia, though the actual basicity depends on solvent and structure.
🔟 Biomolecules

📌 Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that produce them on hydrolysis.

Examples

  • Glucose
  • Fructose
  • Sucrose
  • Starch
  • Cellulose

📌 Proteins

Proteins are polymers of amino acids joined by peptide bonds.

📌 Amino Acids

Amino acids contain both amino (-NH₂) and carboxyl (-COOH) groups.

📌 Nucleic Acids

DNA and RNA are nucleic acids involved in storage and transmission of genetic information.

⭐ Protein → amino acid units ⭐ DNA/RNA → nucleotide units
