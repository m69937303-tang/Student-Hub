1️⃣ Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

1. Introduction

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants involves the formation of male and female gametes, transfer of pollen, fertilisation and formation of seeds and fruits.

2. Flower

A flower is the reproductive structure of angiosperms.

Four Main Whorls

  • Calyx — sepals
  • Corolla — petals
  • Androecium — stamens
  • Gynoecium — carpels

3. Stamen

The stamen is the male reproductive organ. Each stamen consists of:

  • Filament
  • Anther

4. Pollen Grain

Pollen grains represent the male gametophyte at the stage of shedding.

Pollen grain generally has two layers:

Exine: outer hard layer containing sporopollenin.
Intine: inner layer.

5. Microsporogenesis

The formation of microspores from pollen mother cells through meiosis is called microsporogenesis.

Pollen Mother Cell ↓ Meiosis ↓ Four haploid microspores ↓ Pollen grains

6. Gynoecium

The female reproductive part of a flower is called gynoecium. It consists of:

  • Stigma
  • Style
  • Ovary

7. Ovule

Ovules are present inside the ovary. An ovule contains the female gametophyte.

8. Megasporogenesis

Formation of megaspores from the megaspore mother cell through meiosis is called megasporogenesis.

Megaspore Mother Cell ↓ Meiosis ↓ Four megaspores ↓ Usually one functional megaspore ↓ Embryo sac

9. Embryo Sac

The typical mature embryo sac is 7-celled and 8-nucleate.

Structure Number
Egg 1
Synergids 2
Polar nuclei 2
Antipodals 3
⭐ Synergids contain special structures called filiform apparatus which help guide the pollen tube.

10. Pollination

Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma is called pollination.

Types

  • Autogamy
  • Geitonogamy
  • Xenogamy

11. Agents of Pollination

  • Wind
  • Water
  • Insects
  • Birds
  • Other animals

12. Pollen-Pistil Interaction

The pollen grain germinates on a compatible stigma and produces a pollen tube. The pollen tube grows through the style towards the ovule.

13. Fertilisation

Fusion of male and female gametes is called fertilisation.

14. Double Fertilisation

Double fertilisation is a characteristic feature of angiosperms.

Syngamy: Male gamete + egg ↓ Zygote

Triple fusion: Male gamete + two polar nuclei ↓ Primary endosperm nucleus
⭐ Double fertilisation = Syngamy + Triple fusion.

15. Post-Fertilisation Changes

  • Zygote → Embryo
  • Ovule → Seed
  • Ovary → Fruit
  • Ovary wall → Pericarp

2️⃣ Human Reproduction

1. Male Reproductive System

The male reproductive system consists of testes, accessory ducts, accessory glands and external genitalia.

2. Testes

Testes are located in the scrotum. They produce sperm and male sex hormones.

Seminiferous Tubules

Seminiferous tubules are the sites of sperm production.

Sertoli Cells

Sertoli cells provide nourishment and support to developing germ cells.

Leydig Cells

Leydig cells produce androgens, mainly testosterone.

3. Spermatogenesis

Spermatogonia ↓ Primary spermatocyte ↓ Secondary spermatocytes ↓ Spermatids ↓ Spermatozoa

4. Spermiogenesis

Transformation of spermatids into spermatozoa is called spermiogenesis.

5. Spermiation

Release of spermatozoa from Sertoli cells into the lumen of seminiferous tubules is called spermiation.

6. Female Reproductive System

  • Ovaries
  • Oviducts
  • Uterus
  • Cervix
  • Vagina

7. Oogenesis

Oogenesis is the process of formation and development of female gametes.

Oogonia ↓ Primary oocyte ↓ Secondary oocyte ↓ Ovum

8. Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a cyclic reproductive process in human females.

Main Phases

  1. Menstrual phase
  2. Follicular phase
  3. Ovulation
  4. Luteal phase

9. Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of the secondary oocyte from the ovary. A surge in LH triggers ovulation.

⭐ LH surge → Ovulation.

10. Fertilisation

Fertilisation normally occurs in the ampullary-isthmic junction of the fallopian tube.

11. Implantation

The blastocyst becomes attached to the uterine wall. This process is called implantation.

12. Pregnancy

After implantation, the embryo develops inside the uterus. The placenta provides exchange between maternal and foetal systems and also has endocrine functions.

13. Parturition

Parturition means childbirth. Hormonal and mechanical signals contribute to the process.

14. Lactation

After childbirth, mammary glands produce milk. Prolactin is important for milk production and oxytocin helps in milk ejection.

3️⃣ Reproductive Health

1. Reproductive Health

Reproductive health means total well-being in all aspects of reproduction.

2. Population Control

Family planning and awareness can help maintain reproductive health and responsible population management.

3. Contraception

Contraceptive methods are used to prevent unwanted pregnancy.

Natural Methods

  • Periodic abstinence
  • Withdrawal
  • Lactational amenorrhea

Barrier Methods

  • Condoms
  • Diaphragms
  • Cervical caps

Intrauterine Devices

  • Copper-releasing IUDs
  • Hormone-releasing IUDs

Hormonal Methods

Hormonal contraceptives can prevent ovulation and alter conditions necessary for fertilisation.

4. Surgical Methods

Vasectomy and tubectomy are permanent contraceptive procedures.

5. Sexually Transmitted Infections

Some infections can spread through sexual contact. Examples include:

  • Gonorrhoea
  • Syphilis
  • Genital herpes
  • Chlamydia
  • HIV infection
⭐ Awareness, prevention, testing and appropriate medical care are important for reproductive health.

6. Infertility

Inability to conceive despite appropriate attempts may be associated with several biological or medical factors.

7. Assisted Reproductive Technologies

  • IVF
  • ZIFT
  • GIFT

4️⃣ Principles of Inheritance and Variation

1. Genetics

Genetics is the study of heredity and variation.

2. Heredity

Heredity is the transmission of characters from parents to offspring.

3. Mendel

Gregor Mendel performed experiments on pea plants and established important principles of inheritance.

4. Monohybrid Cross

A cross involving one pair of contrasting characters is called a monohybrid cross.

Example: TT × tt ↓ F₁ = all Tt

5. Law of Dominance

In a heterozygote, one allele may express itself while the other is masked.

6. Law of Segregation

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete receives only one allele.

⭐ Alleles do not blend permanently. They segregate during gamete formation.

7. Dihybrid Cross

A cross involving two pairs of contrasting characters is called a dihybrid cross.

Classical F₂ phenotypic ratio: 9 : 3 : 3 : 1

8. Test Cross

A test cross is performed between an individual with an unknown genotype and a homozygous recessive individual.

9. Incomplete Dominance

Neither allele completely dominates the other, resulting in an intermediate phenotype in the heterozygote.

10. Codominance

Both alleles express themselves in the heterozygote. Example: AB blood group.

11. Multiple Alleles

A gene may have more than two allelic forms in a population. The ABO blood group system is a classic example.

12. Sex Determination

In humans, females generally have XX chromosomes and males generally have XY chromosomes.

13. Sex-Linked Inheritance

Genes located on sex chromosomes may show sex-linked inheritance. Examples include haemophilia and colour blindness.

14. Mutation

A mutation is a sudden heritable change in genetic material.

5️⃣ Molecular Basis of Inheritance

1. DNA

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It stores hereditary information in most organisms.

2. Structure of DNA

DNA consists of two polynucleotide chains arranged in a double helix.

Nitrogen Bases

  • Adenine — A
  • Thymine — T
  • Guanine — G
  • Cytosine — C
A pairs with T.
G pairs with C.

3. Hydrogen Bonds

A–T → 2 hydrogen bonds
G–C → 3 hydrogen bonds

4. DNA Replication

DNA replication is the process by which DNA makes an identical copy of itself.

DNA ↓ Unwinding ↓ Template strands ↓ Complementary nucleotides ↓ Two DNA molecules

5. Semi-Conservative Replication

Each daughter DNA molecule contains one parental strand and one newly synthesised strand.

6. Transcription

Transcription is the formation of RNA using DNA as a template.

DNA ↓ RNA

7. Translation

Translation is the synthesis of protein using the information present in mRNA.

mRNA ↓ Ribosome ↓ Amino acid sequence ↓ Protein

8. Genetic Code

The genetic code is the sequence of nucleotides in mRNA that determines the amino acid sequence of a protein.

⭐ AUG is the common start codon.
⭐ UAA, UAG and UGA are stop codons.

9. Lac Operon

The lac operon is an example of gene regulation in bacteria. It controls genes involved in lactose utilisation.

10. Human Genome Project

The Human Genome Project aimed to determine the sequence of bases in the human genome and identify genes.

11. DNA Fingerprinting

DNA fingerprinting identifies individuals based on differences in specific DNA regions.

6️⃣ Evolution

1. Evolution

Evolution refers to the gradual change in populations over generations.

2. Origin of Life

Scientific explanations for the origin of life involve chemical evolution and gradual development of complex molecules and living systems.

3. Evidence of Evolution

  • Fossils
  • Comparative anatomy
  • Embryology
  • Molecular evidence

4. Darwin's Theory

Darwin proposed natural selection as an important mechanism of evolution.

5. Natural Selection

Individuals with advantageous heritable variations may survive and reproduce more successfully under particular environmental conditions.

6. Adaptive Radiation

Evolution of different species from a common ancestral stock in different habitats is called adaptive radiation.

7. Hardy-Weinberg Principle

In an ideal population, allele frequencies remain constant from generation to generation when evolutionary forces are absent.

p + q = 1

p² + 2pq + q² = 1

8. Factors Affecting Genetic Equilibrium

  • Gene migration
  • Genetic drift
  • Mutation
  • Genetic recombination
  • Natural selection

🔥 Important Questions

1. Explain Darwin's theory of natural selection.
2. What is adaptive radiation?
3. Explain Hardy-Weinberg principle.
7️⃣ Human Health and Disease

1. Health

Health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being.

2. Disease

Disease is a condition that negatively affects normal functioning of the body.

3. Pathogens

Disease-causing organisms are called pathogens.

  • Bacteria
  • Viruses
  • Protozoans
  • Fungi
  • Helminths

4. Immunity

Immunity is the ability of the body to resist or fight disease-causing agents.

Innate Immunity

Present from birth and provides non-specific defence.

Acquired Immunity

Develops during life and is specific to particular antigens.

5. Antibodies

Antibodies are proteins produced by B-lymphocytes in response to specific antigens.

6. Vaccination

Vaccination stimulates the immune system and helps develop immunological memory.

7. AIDS

AIDS is caused by HIV, which attacks important cells of the immune system.

8. Cancer

Cancer involves uncontrolled abnormal cell growth and division.

9. Allergies

Allergy is an exaggerated immune response to certain normally harmless substances.

10. Drugs and Alcohol

Substance misuse can affect physical and mental health and may cause dependence and other serious problems.

8️⃣ Microbes in Human Welfare

1. Microorganisms

Microorganisms are microscopic organisms that are found in almost every environment.

2. Household Products

  • Lactobacillus → curd
  • Yeast → bread and fermented beverages

3. Industrial Products

  • Alcohol
  • Organic acids
  • Enzymes
  • Antibiotics

4. Antibiotics

Antibiotics are substances produced by microorganisms that can inhibit or kill other microorganisms.

5. Sewage Treatment

Microorganisms play an important role in biological treatment of sewage.

6. Biogas

Methanogenic microorganisms produce methane-rich biogas from organic waste under anaerobic conditions.

7. Biofertilisers

Microorganisms that improve nutrient availability to plants are called biofertilisers.

8. Biocontrol

Biological agents can be used to control pests and pathogens.

⭐ Microbes are useful in food production, industry, waste treatment, medicine and agriculture.
9️⃣ Biotechnology: Principles and Processes

1. Biotechnology

Biotechnology involves using living organisms, cells or biological systems to develop useful products and processes.

2. Recombinant DNA Technology

Recombinant DNA technology allows DNA fragments from different sources to be joined and introduced into a suitable host.

3. Important Tools

  • Restriction enzymes
  • DNA ligase
  • Vectors
  • Host cells
  • Polymerases

4. Restriction Enzymes

Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific recognition sequences.

5. DNA Ligase

DNA ligase joins DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds.

6. Vector

A vector carries foreign DNA into a host cell. Plasmids are commonly used vectors.

7. PCR

Polymerase Chain Reaction is used to amplify a specific DNA sequence.

PCR involves repeated cycles of:

Denaturation ↓ Annealing ↓ Extension

8. Gel Electrophoresis

DNA fragments can be separated based on size by gel electrophoresis. Smaller fragments generally move faster through the gel.

9. Bioreactors

Bioreactors provide suitable conditions for large-scale production using biological systems.

🔟 Biotechnology and its Applications

1. Applications of Biotechnology

  • Medicine
  • Agriculture
  • Industry
  • Environmental management

2. Genetically Modified Organisms

GM organisms contain genetic material that has been deliberately altered using biotechnology.

3. Bt Crops

Bt crops contain genes derived from Bacillus thuringiensis that provide protection against certain insect pests.

4. Recombinant Insulin

Recombinant DNA technology can be used to produce human insulin using microorganisms.

5. Gene Therapy

Gene therapy aims to treat certain disorders by introducing, replacing or modifying genetic material in cells.

6. Molecular Diagnosis

Techniques such as PCR can help detect specific genetic sequences associated with pathogens or diseases.

7. Ethical Issues

Biotechnology raises questions involving biosafety, environmental impact, genetic privacy and responsible use.

1️⃣1️⃣ Organisms and Populations

1. Ecology

Ecology is the study of interactions among organisms and between organisms and their environment.

2. Population

A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in a particular geographical area.

3. Population Attributes

  • Population density
  • Birth rate
  • Death rate
  • Sex ratio
  • Age distribution

4. Population Growth

Population size changes due to births, deaths, immigration and emigration.

5. Exponential Growth

dN/dt = rN

6. Logistic Growth

When resources are limited, population growth may follow a logistic pattern.

dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K

7. Interactions

Interaction Effect
Mutualism +/+
Competition −/−
Predation +/−
Parasitism +/−
Commensalism +/0
⭐ Population ecology is important for understanding how organisms interact with limited environmental resources.
1️⃣2️⃣ Ecosystem

1. Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of living organisms interacting with each other and with their physical environment.

2. Components

  • Biotic components
  • Abiotic components

3. Producers

Green plants and other autotrophs produce organic matter using external energy sources.

4. Consumers

Consumers obtain energy by feeding on other organisms.

5. Decomposers

Bacteria and fungi break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients.

6. Food Chain

Plants ↓ Herbivores ↓ Carnivores ↓ Higher carnivores

7. Food Web

A network of interconnected food chains is called a food web.

8. Energy Flow

Energy flows through an ecosystem in one direction.

9. Ecological Pyramids

  • Pyramid of number
  • Pyramid of biomass
  • Pyramid of energy
⭐ Pyramid of energy is always upright because energy is lost at each trophic transfer.

10. Decomposition

Decomposition is the breakdown of complex organic matter into simpler substances.

1️⃣3️⃣ Biodiversity and Conservation

1. Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms present on Earth.

2. Levels of Biodiversity

  • Genetic diversity
  • Species diversity
  • Ecosystem diversity

3. Importance of Biodiversity

  • Ecosystem stability
  • Food resources
  • Medicinal resources
  • Economic value
  • Scientific value

4. Biodiversity Loss

Major causes include habitat loss, overexploitation, invasive species and co-extinctions.

5. Conservation

In-situ Conservation

Conservation of species in their natural habitats.

Ex-situ Conservation

Conservation outside natural habitats, such as zoos, botanical gardens and seed banks.

⭐ National parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves are examples associated with in-situ conservation.

1️⃣4️⃣ Environmental Issues

1. Pollution

Pollution is an undesirable change in the physical, chemical or biological characteristics of the environment.

2. Air Pollution

Air pollution results from harmful gases, particulate matter and other pollutants entering the atmosphere.

3. Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate water bodies.

4. Eutrophication

Excess nutrients entering water bodies can cause excessive growth of algae and other organisms. This is called eutrophication.

5. Biomagnification

The concentration of certain persistent pollutants can increase at successive trophic levels.

6. Greenhouse Effect

Certain gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, contributing to warming of the Earth's atmosphere.

7. Ozone Depletion

Reduction in stratospheric ozone increases the amount of harmful ultraviolet radiation reaching Earth's surface.

8. Solid Waste

Proper segregation, recycling, treatment and responsible disposal help reduce solid waste problems.

9. Sustainable Development

Sustainable development aims to meet present needs while protecting the ability of future generations to meet their needs.
