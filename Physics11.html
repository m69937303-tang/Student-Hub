1️⃣ Units and Measurements

1. Physical Quantity

A physical quantity is a quantity that can be measured and expressed using a numerical value and a unit.

2. SI System

The International System of Units is the standard system used for scientific measurements.

Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length metre m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Temperature kelvin K
Electric current ampere A
Amount of substance mole mol
Luminous intensity candela cd

3. Dimensions

Dimensions express a physical quantity in terms of fundamental quantities such as mass, length and time.

Velocity = [LT⁻¹]

Acceleration = [LT⁻²]

Force = [MLT⁻²]

Work = [ML²T⁻²]

Power = [ML²T⁻³]

4. Significant Figures

Significant figures indicate the meaningful digits in a measured quantity.

  • All non-zero digits are significant.
  • Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.
  • Leading zeros are generally not significant.

5. Errors

Measurements may contain errors due to limitations of instruments or experimental conditions.

Absolute error = |Measured value − True value|
⭐ Dimensional analysis can be used to check the dimensional consistency of an equation.

Important Questions

1. What are fundamental and derived quantities?
2. Explain dimensional analysis.
3. What are significant figures?
2️⃣ Motion in a Straight Line

1. Motion

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to a reference point with time.

2. Distance

Distance is the total path length travelled by an object. It is a scalar quantity.

3. Displacement

Displacement is the shortest directed distance between initial and final positions.

Distance is scalar.
Displacement is vector.

4. Speed

Speed = Distance / Time

5. Velocity

Velocity = Displacement / Time

6. Acceleration

a = (v − u) / t

7. Equations of Motion

v = u + at

s = ut + ½at²

v² = u² + 2as

s = ((u + v)/2)t

Here u is initial velocity, v is final velocity, a is acceleration, t is time and s is displacement.

8. Free Fall

When an object falls under the influence of gravity alone, its acceleration is approximately g near Earth's surface.

g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

Important Questions

1. Differentiate distance and displacement.
2. Derive the three equations of motion.
3. Explain velocity-time graph.
3️⃣ Motion in a Plane

1. Scalars and Vectors

A scalar has magnitude only, whereas a vector has both magnitude and direction.

Scalar Vector
Mass Displacement
Time Velocity
Speed Acceleration

2. Vector Addition

Vectors can be added using the triangle law or parallelogram law.

3. Components of Vector

Aₓ = A cosθ

Aᵧ = A sinθ

4. Projectile Motion

A projectile is an object projected into air that moves under gravity while neglecting air resistance.

Time of flight: T = 2u sinθ / g

Maximum height: H = u²sin²θ / 2g

Range: R = u²sin2θ / g

5. Uniform Circular Motion

In uniform circular motion, speed remains constant but velocity changes continuously because its direction changes.

Centripetal acceleration: a = v²/r
⭐ In circular motion, acceleration is directed towards the centre of the circle.

Important Questions

1. Explain projectile motion.
2. Derive the expression for maximum height.
3. Explain centripetal acceleration.
4️⃣ Laws of Motion

1. Force

Force is an interaction that can change the state of motion or shape of an object.

2. Newton's First Law

An object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.

3. Newton's Second Law

F = ma

More generally, force is related to the rate of change of momentum.

4. Newton's Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

5. Momentum

p = mv

6. Impulse

Impulse = Force × Time J = FΔt

7. Friction

Friction opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion between surfaces.

Limiting friction: f = μN

8. Circular Motion

Centripetal force: F = mv²/r
⭐ Action and reaction forces act on different objects.

Important Questions

1. State and explain Newton's three laws.
2. Define momentum and impulse.
3. Explain friction.
5️⃣ Work, Energy and Power

1. Work

Work is done when a force causes displacement of an object in the direction having a component along the force.

W = Fs cosθ

2. Kinetic Energy

K = ½mv²

3. Potential Energy

Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is:

U = mgh

4. Work-Energy Theorem

The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.

Wnet = ΔK

5. Power

Power = Work / Time

P = W/t

6. Mechanical Energy

Mechanical Energy = Kinetic Energy + Potential Energy

7. Conservation of Energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only change from one form to another.

Important Questions

1. Derive kinetic energy.
2. Explain work-energy theorem.
3. Explain conservation of mechanical energy.
6️⃣ System of Particles and Rotational Motion

1. Centre of Mass

The centre of mass is the point at which the entire mass of a system may be considered concentrated for describing translational motion.

For two particles: xcm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂)/(m₁ + m₂)

2. Torque

τ = r × F

Magnitude: τ = rF sinθ

3. Angular Velocity

ω = Δθ/Δt

4. Angular Acceleration

α = Δω/Δt

5. Moment of Inertia

Moment of inertia measures resistance of a body to change in its rotational motion.

I = Σmr²

6. Angular Momentum

L = Iω

7. Rotational Kinetic Energy

K = ½Iω²

8. Rolling Motion

For pure rolling without slipping:

v = Rω
⭐ Torque plays a role in rotational motion similar to the role of force in translational motion.

Important Questions

1. Define centre of mass.
2. Define torque and moment of inertia.
3. Explain angular momentum.
7️⃣ Gravitation

1. Universal Law of Gravitation

Every two masses attract each other with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

F = Gm₁m₂/r²

2. Acceleration Due to Gravity

g = GM/R²

3. Gravitational Potential

V = −GM/r

4. Gravitational Potential Energy

U = −GMm/r

5. Escape Velocity

Escape velocity is the minimum speed required for an object to escape the gravitational influence of a celestial body without further propulsion.

ve = √(2GM/R)

6. Orbital Velocity

vo = √(GM/r)

7. Satellites

Artificial satellites can be placed in orbit around Earth for communication, navigation, weather observation and scientific research.

⭐ Escape velocity from Earth is independent of the mass of the escaping object when air resistance is neglected.

Important Questions

1. State Newton's law of gravitation.
2. Derive escape velocity.
3. Differentiate orbital velocity and escape velocity.
8️⃣ Mechanical Properties of Solids

1. Elasticity

Elasticity is the property by which a body tends to regain its original shape and size after removal of deforming force, within its elastic limit.

2. Stress

Stress = Force / Area

3. Strain

Strain = Change in dimension / Original dimension

Strain is dimensionless.

4. Hooke's Law

Stress ∝ Strain

5. Young's Modulus

Y = Longitudinal stress / Longitudinal strain

6. Bulk Modulus

Bulk modulus describes resistance to change in volume.

7. Shear Modulus

Shear modulus describes resistance to change in shape under tangential stress.

⭐ Stress has dimensions of pressure.
⭐ Strain has no dimensions.

Important Questions

1. State Hooke's law.
2. Define stress and strain.
3. Explain Young's modulus.
9️⃣ Mechanical Properties of Fluids

1. Pressure

P = F/A

2. Pascal's Law

Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions.

3. Archimedes' Principle

An object immersed in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

4. Equation of Continuity

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

5. Bernoulli's Principle

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

6. Viscosity

Viscosity is the internal resistance offered by a fluid to relative motion between its layers.

7. Surface Tension

Surface tension arises from cohesive forces between molecules at a liquid surface.

8. Stokes' Law

F = 6πηrv

Important Questions

1. Explain Pascal's law.
2. Explain Archimedes' principle.
3. Derive Bernoulli's equation.
🔟 Thermal Properties of Matter

1. Temperature

Temperature indicates the degree of hotness or coldness of a body and is related to the average kinetic energy of its particles.

2. Celsius and Kelvin

K = °C + 273.15

3. Thermal Expansion

Most substances expand when heated, though the amount depends on the material and conditions.

Linear expansion: ΔL = αLΔT

4. Heat

Q = mcΔT

5. Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat capacity is the heat required to raise the temperature of unit mass of a substance by one degree.

6. Latent Heat

Q = mL

7. Heat Transfer

  • Conduction
  • Convection
  • Radiation

8. Newton's Law of Cooling

Under suitable conditions, the rate of cooling is related to the temperature difference between the body and surroundings.

Important Questions

1. Define specific heat capacity.
2. Explain thermal expansion.
3. Differentiate conduction, convection and radiation.
1️⃣1️⃣ Thermodynamics

1. System and Surroundings

The part of the universe chosen for thermodynamic study is called the system. Everything outside it is the surroundings.

2. Internal Energy

Internal energy is the total microscopic energy associated with the particles of a system.

3. Zeroth Law

If two systems are separately in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

4. First Law of Thermodynamics

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW

Heat supplied to a system is used to change internal energy and/or perform work.

5. Isothermal Process

Temperature remains constant.

For ideal gas: PV = constant

6. Adiabatic Process

No heat is exchanged with the surroundings.

PVᵞ = constant

7. Second Law

The second law introduces the direction of natural processes and the concept of entropy.

⭐ Isothermal → Temperature constant.
⭐ Adiabatic → Heat exchange zero.

Important Questions

1. State the first law of thermodynamics.
2. Differentiate isothermal and adiabatic processes.
3. Explain the second law of thermodynamics.
1️⃣2️⃣ Kinetic Theory

1. Kinetic Theory

Kinetic theory explains macroscopic properties of gases using the motion of their microscopic particles.

2. Ideal Gas

PV = nRT

3. Pressure

Gas pressure arises from collisions of molecules with the walls of the container.

4. Kinetic Energy

Average translational kinetic energy ∝ Temperature

5. Temperature

Absolute temperature is directly related to the average translational kinetic energy of ideal-gas molecules.

⭐ At the same temperature, ideal-gas molecules of different gases have the same average translational kinetic energy.

Important Questions

1. Explain kinetic theory of gases.
2. Derive the pressure relation from kinetic theory.
3. State ideal gas equation.
1️⃣3️⃣ Oscillations

1. Periodic Motion

A motion that repeats itself after equal intervals of time is called periodic motion.

2. Simple Harmonic Motion

In SHM, restoring acceleration is directly proportional to displacement and directed towards the mean position.

a = −ω²x

3. Displacement

x = A sin(ωt + φ)

4. Time Period

T = 2π/ω

5. Frequency

f = 1/T

6. Simple Pendulum

For small oscillations, the time period of a simple pendulum is approximately:

T = 2π√(L/g)

7. Energy in SHM

The total mechanical energy remains constant in ideal SHM, while kinetic and potential energies interchange continuously.

⭐ At mean position: kinetic energy is maximum.
⭐ At extreme position: potential energy is maximum.

Important Questions

1. Define SHM.
2. Derive time period of simple pendulum.
3. Explain energy changes in SHM.
1️⃣4️⃣ Waves

1. Wave

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without requiring bulk transport of matter.

2. Types of Waves

  • Transverse waves
  • Longitudinal waves

3. Wavelength

The distance between two successive points in the same phase is called wavelength.

λ = wavelength

4. Frequency

f = 1/T

5. Wave Speed

v = fλ

6. Sound Waves

Sound in air is generally a longitudinal mechanical wave. It requires a material medium for propagation.

7. Superposition

When waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of individual displacements.

8. Standing Waves

Standing waves are formed by superposition of two waves of the same frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions.

9. Beats

Beat frequency = |f₁ − f₂|
⭐ Mechanical waves require a material medium.

Important Questions

1. Differentiate transverse and longitudinal waves.
2. Derive v = fλ.
3. Explain standing waves.
4. What are beats?
